# What a Klondike Derby sled is (research, 2026-10-11)

There is no single national BSA spec. Each council or district publishes its own leader guide. The guides below agree on the core idea, and differ on the details that matter most to us: wheels, size and hardware.

## The event
- A patrol of 4–8 scouts moves its sled from "town" to "town" (skill stations). It is a timed run between stations.
- **Roles:**
  - **Rider:** one scout rides in the sled.
  - **Musher or driver:** one scout at the back pushes, steers and directs. The role rotates.
  - **Dog team:** the rest pull from the front on a rope harness.
- **Harness safety:** hand loops are about 6–9" long, so a loop can never go over a neck, shoulder or waist. Their stated reason is to avoid strangulation and being trapped under a runaway sled.
- **Race rules:** some events require every scout to keep contact with the sled, and allow at most 8 pulling.
- **Bert Adams / AAC (our handout, `reference/01-rules-handout.jpg`):**
  - the sled must carry at least one scout throughout;
  - wheels may be used between towns;
  - carry the 10 Essentials;
  - it is a timed race;
  - the start is about 7 pm (dark).

## The sled
- **Form:** home-made, and "of a design similar to a dog sled". Toboggans and wagons are banned in several councils.
- **Size:**
  - **Length:** about 6 ft (range 5–6.5 ft).
  - **Width:** 18" (range 18–24").
  - **Height:** about 36–40" at the rear handlebar.
- **Anatomy (dog sled):**
  - **Runners:** two long runners, often old downhill skis (185 cm or longer) or ash or hardwood, with the front curled up.
  - **Stanchions:** short uprights from the runners to a slatted basket floor.
  - **Brush bow:** a curved bumper joining the runner tips.
  - **Side rails:** run over the uprights, with canvas or netting to keep gear in.
  - **Handlebow:** at the back, on tall rear stanchions with braces.
  - **Tow bar or bridle:** at the front.
- **Materials:**
  - wood framing, often required, with "hardwood, pine is too soft";
  - glue and screws or lashings;
  - some councils ban metal brackets and nails, and require youth to do the building.
- **Wheels: council rules vary widely.**
  - Allowed freely in some.
  - Allowed but removable for the race in others.
  - Limited to 6 wheels under 12" diameter, or no more than 6" of lift, in others.
  - Banned in a few.
  - The usual approach is a **removable wheel kit** that bolts to the runners, so the same sled runs on snow.
- **Weight:** not specified in the guides. These sleds are light enough for a patrol to carry; the plans suggest roughly 30–60 lb.

## What this means for our design
1. **The rider is a passenger.** They sit or kneel in the basket. There is no rider steering and no "drive it downhill" mode. The **musher at the back steers and brakes** with the handlebow, and the team pulls.
2. **It should look like a dog sled.** That means:
   - a low basket on stanchions;
   - long runners curling up at the front, with a brush bow;
   - a tall rear handlebow;
   - open slatted sides with a gear curtain.

   It should not look like a boat hull or a cart body.
3. **It should be narrow and light:** 18–24" wide, about 6 ft long, with a target closer to 40–60 lb than 174 lb.
4. **Wheels are an add-on to a sled,** not the basis of a cart. Small wheels (≤ 12" in some rules) bolt to the runners. Steering comes from the team's pull, with the musher pushing and skidding the back.
5. **Confirm the AAC 2026 sled rules** before freezing anything: size, wheel limits, hardware or lashing rules, and youth-built rules.
